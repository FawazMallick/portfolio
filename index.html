Who Am I

I moved to Dubai, U.A.E. during my first year of life, where I grew up and spent most of my time. Admittedly, although my dreams were big, circumstances led me to believe I lacked the competence required to pursue engineering or science. At 17, after much effort from my mom and uncle, and with generous funding from my father, I came to the U.S. to study. My family hoped the change would inspire something in me, while I was skeptical. But as fate would have it, coming to the U.S. and its education system awoke in me a level of motivation and confidence I never thought I would achieve. I went from a student who barely passed his high school exams to someone who graduated in biomedical engineering while maintaining part-time jobs and other obligations.

Post graduation, I was left with a hunger for more, which brought me to pursue my master's at Carnegie Mellon: my dream school, with a brand for churning out competent and skilled experts in any field. As I look towards completing my master's, I envision myself living a life of abundance, where my career goals to be a scientist and engineer are fulfilled, my dream of having a Ferrari has been realized, and where my family and friends surround me with love and happiness.

In the meantime, I spend my time building up the personality and skills I see myself having. During my leisure time I play chess, go on walks, or take part in extracurricular clubs on campus to stay social and active. This portfolio, far from comprehensive, shows you a small glimpse of my professional and academic life so far.

Research Projects

Surgical Robotics

Can robots be useful tools during surgery?

Femur fractures are a significant problem resulting from high-impact collisions such as car crashes. The femur being the strongest bone, surrounded by dense muscles, means surgeons have a hard time realigning the bone manually during fracture realignment surgery. This work explores algorithm development to be deployed on an already developed fracture alignment robotic system.

Wearable Robotics · Published

Exoskeletons make good walking aids?... Maybe

Falls are a leading cause of injury amongst the elderly and those with gait impairments. This work explored whether a sufficient hardware system can be developed to measure biomechanical interactions between a human and an exoskeleton device, while also testing those cues against a customized simulation model.
